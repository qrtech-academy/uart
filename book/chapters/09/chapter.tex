\chapter{The Real Transport, and Both Toolchains}
\label{c9:ch}

{\large\itshape The bottom layer becomes real; nothing above the seam changes, and both chips get
programmed.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The AVR's own SPI peripheral}: \code{SPCR}, \code{SPSR} and \code{SPDR} as genuine
    memory-mapped \code{volatile} registers, configured as an SPI master at
    f\textsubscript{osc}/16, which is 1\,MHz, mode 0, most significant bit first, matching Part~3 of
    the protocol (\secref{proto:part3}).
  \item \textbf{\code{AvrSpi} on top of it}, implementing the same \code{driver::transport::Interface}
    seam that the \code{driver::transport::Stub} of \chapref{8} did, only now clocking real bytes.
    Nothing above the seam changes, which is the whole return on the design work of \chapref{6}.
  \item \textbf{What a freestanding target removes}, namely the heap, exceptions, RTTI and
    iostreams, and what replaces each: static storage, return codes, virtual dispatch in place of
    \code{dynamic_cast}, and logging over the AVR's own hardware UART.
  \item \textbf{Building with avr-gcc and flashing with avrdude.}
  \item \textbf{The other toolchain}: synthesizing \file{uart_board.vhd} in Quartus, assigning pins,
    and programming the DE0-CV over USB-Blaster. Both halves of the course reach real hardware in
    this chapter, and doing the FPGA side here rather than on bring-up day means a toolchain problem
    surfaces while there is still time to solve it.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

This is where the stub gives way to real hardware. After this chapter you should be able to
configure the AVR SPI master registers correctly for the transport contract, and explain each bit
they set; implement \code{AvrSpi} so that the existing driver and its host tests are reused
unchanged; explain which host-side idioms do not survive a freestanding build, and what replaces
them; and synthesize and program the DE0-CV, and read a fit and timing report well enough to say
whether the design met timing at 50\,MHz.

\textbf{Before you start}, you need the \code{driver::transport::Interface} seam of \chapref{6},
which is what the new transport implements, plus the \code{Uart} driver of \chapref{7} and the host
tests of \chapref{8}, both reused here unchanged. You also need the finished \code{uart_top} of
\chapref{5}, passing its system testbench, because the second half of the chapter puts that design
on the FPGA. From embedded C the chapter assumes the AVR toolchain, avr-gcc and avrdude, and
register-level programming; from modern embedded C++, the \code{volatile} semantics of memory-mapped
registers. Install \textbf{Quartus Prime Lite} before you start: it is a multi-gigabyte download,
and it is the one tool the book has not needed until now.

Read \secref{c9:sec:avr_transport} for the SPI peripheral and \code{AvrSpi}, and
\secref{c9:sec:freestanding} for the freestanding target and the toolchain. Alongside them, read the
ATmega328P datasheet's SPI section covering \code{SPCR}, \code{SPSR} and \code{SPDR}, and the
provided \file{avr/} runtime and Makefile under \file{fw/} (\file{fw/README.md}).

Write \code{AvrSpi}, confirm that the host tests still pass unchanged with \code{make build-cpp},
then cross-compile and flash a minimal bring-up \code{main} to see the toolchain end to end:

\begin{shell}
make -C fw/avr flash # avr-gcc + avrdude; see fw/README.md.
\end{shell}

\noindent \code{AvrSpi} is about forty lines; go through \code{SPCR} bit by bit as you write it,
because a wrong bit here is a bug you would otherwise meet on a logic analyzer at the bench.

Then take the same journey on the other chip: synthesize the \code{uart_top} finished in
\chapref{5} through the provided \file{uart_board.vhd} wrapper, read the fit and timing report, and
program the DE0-CV over USB-Blaster. What the board can show on its own is the data-plane pin
loopback: with the wrapper's \code{rx} pin wired straight to its \code{tx} pin, bypassing
\code{uart_top}, a terminal on the USB-serial adapter echoes what you type. The peripheral itself
cannot be exercised yet: it transmits only what an SPI master writes to \code{TX_DATA}, and that
master is the Nano, in \chapref{10}.

The exercises in \secref{c9:sec:exercises} implement \code{AvrSpi} (Exercise~\ref{c9:ex:1}) and the
freestanding bring-up \code{main} (Exercise~\ref{c9:ex:2}), add logging over the AVR's own hardware
UART and work out one \code{readReg()} round trip against the 1\,MHz \code{SCK} budget, measuring it
on a logic analyzer if you have one (Exercise~\ref{c9:ex:3}). Synthesize and program your own
\code{uart_top} as well (Exercise~\ref{c9:ex:4}), so that both chips are running your code before you
reach the bench.
